## About

Rafikh Rashid Shaikh is a member of the [Gnowledge Lab](/index.html) and a researcher in computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL). His work has centred on what happens when computers in a classroom *share their screens* — turning the individual workstation into a shared memory surface that mediates conversation, attention, and accelerated learning.

A long thread of his research, carried out with [Nagarjuna G.](/team/nagarjuna-g.html), used an instant-messaging application running on networked computers (including OLPC deployments) in a rural school at Khairat, to support both numeracy and literacy in primary-school children. The studies — reported across epiSTEME, CSCL, ICME and *Education and Information Technologies* — showed that an externalised shared memory in a collaborative, game-like setting can both accelerate learning and sustain motivation. See the [Shared Memory Space](/projects/research.html#shared-memory-space) research thread for the framing.

He has also worked on **embodied and interactive approaches to learning basic astronomy**, and co-authored the early *Slate for every child* essay with [Amit Dhakulkar](/team/amit-dhakulkar.html) on rethinking education in the age of computers.

Beyond his research, Rafikh suggested the name **naYana** (a phonetic palindrome — nYn — that in Sanskrit means *eye*) for the lab's [naYana](/projects/naYana.html) phonetic alphabet project.

He is also an active promoter of [**Foldscope**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foldscope) in India — the origami paper microscope developed by Manu Prakash's group at Stanford — helping bring frugal, durable, sub-dollar microscopy into school and undergraduate classrooms and into the lab's investigative-biology and home-lab work.

## Research interests

- Computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL) in school classrooms
- Shared-screen / shared-memory mediation of learning and conversation
- Instant messaging and chat as constructionist learning contexts
- Numeracy and literacy in primary schools, including in rural / OLPC settings
- Embodied and interactive approaches to science (e.g. basic astronomy)
- Zone of Proximal Development in connected, networked learning environments
